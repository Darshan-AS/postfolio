## 4. Avatar, name and phone travel to the customer's screen (`271c9f5`, `3e75d1a`)
A tap on a customer's row sends the row's avatar, name and phone number to the head of the customer's screen; the back button sends them back. The route is still `go_router`'s page, with its URL, its page transition and the back swipe.

**Why the whole-screen morph of 2026-11-17 looked wrong.** Under a `Hero` both pages still run the theme's transition (the old page fades to the page colour and slides), and the framework hides the destination and draws a copy above both pages. One hero the size of the screen therefore covered the list at once. And the screen arrived as a skeleton, because a detail screen fetches its full record when it opens.

**What was built.**
- `EntityHero` (`lib/core/widgets/layout/entity_hero.dart`) wraps one part in the framework's `Hero`, tagged with the entity and the part. `EntityListTile.heroTag` and `EntityDetailHeader.heroTag` carry the avatar and the name. The customer's row and screen wrap the phone number themselves. In flight it draws the screen's copy, scaled to the box: laid out in a box of another size, a name would wrap or be cut.
- The head of the screen is real on the first frame. `customerInListProvider(id)` reads the customer out of the loaded list, `AsyncSingleEntityBuilder.seed` builds the screen from it at once, and `EntityDetailScaffold.bodyLoading` draws the sections as a placeholder until the full record (savings account, nominees) arrives. The head is never inside a `Skeletonizer`: it rebuilds its child as a new widget when it is switched off, and when a destination is replaced mid-flight the framework drops the flight and shows two copies.
- `EntityHeroFollower` holds what the head shows and the row does not (the email line, Call / SMS / WhatsApp). It fades in over the last 30% of the screen's transition (`AppMotion.afterSharedElements`) and out over the first 30% on the way back. Without it the avatar passed over the Call button and the name over the email line, both already faded in. **This is about 25 lines of our own motion code**, on the framework's route animation; the rule is to ask the owner first, and it is flagged for the owner's look.
- On a back swipe nothing travels: the page follows the finger as before, and the follower stays drawn. `Hero.transitionOnUserGestures` is left off because this Flutter restarts the transition from its end when the swipe is committed, which would make the parts jump (read in `routes.dart`, not run).
- A device set to remove animations gets neither.

**Live page transitions (`271c9f5`).** Android's transition animates a picture of each screen, taken on its first frame. Measured in the rendered frames: the new screen's sections, which fade in, stayed blank for the whole transition, and the tapped row kept its avatar and name for about 100 ms after they had left. The theme's builder now has `allowSnapshotting: false`. It applies to every page transition on Android. The cost on a slow phone has not been measured.

- Opened from a deposit's customer link nothing travels (the deposit's screen has no tagged parts), yet the email line and the actions still come in last.

## 3. A tag names the row, not just the entity
Read in `Hero._allHeroesFor` (`widgets/heroes.dart`): for a transition the framework collects the heroes under the screen being left, including the current page of every navigator nested in it. `go_router` keeps each opened tab alive, out of view. With a tag made of the deposit's id, opening a customer from the Customers tab paired the deposit's row in the One-Time tab with the same deposit's row on the customer's screen, and sent it there. A test confirms it: `a row in a tab out of view sends nothing to the customer's screen` fails when both rows are given one tag.
- `EntityHero.tagFrom(origin, id)`: the origin says where the row is. `EntityHero.ownList` for an entity's own list; the customer's screen has its own for its deposit rows.
- The row hands its origin to the route it pushes, as `$extra` on `CustomerDetailRoute`, `OneTimeDepositDetailRoute` and `RecurringDepositDetailRoute`; the screen takes it as `openedFrom` and builds its head's tag from it. It is a short string, so `go_router` keeps it when it serialises the route; it is not in the URL, and a refresh loses it, which costs the animation only.
- A screen that no row opened has no tag. Nothing travels to it and its head does not wait. In part 1 a customer's screen opened from a deposit's link still held its email line and actions back for a flight that did not happen.
- `EntityDetailHeader` now puts its supporting lines in the follower itself, with the badge, the footer and the actions. A screen marks only what travels.
- `OneTimeDepositCard` and `RecurringDepositCard`: `overrideTitle` / `overrideSubtitle`, which had one use, became `titleByAccount`, so the row knows which of its lines is the account number.

## 4. The deposit screens start from the list's record

# Part 4 — The back swipe and the travelling parts

## Objective
On the Pixel 11 the parts travel back when the app bar's back button is pressed and not on a back swipe. The owner asked how predictive back and the travelling parts fit together, and whether there is an easy built-in way or a complicated patch. A question: nothing was changed in the code.

## How the two use the same animation
Read in `material_ui`'s `predictive_back_page_transitions_builder.dart` and the framework's `routes.dart` and `heroes.dart`:
- During a back swipe the route's animation follows the finger (`1 - progress`), and the page shrinks with it. When the swipe is released, `_handleDragEnd` pops the route and restarts that animation from its end (`reverse(from: upperBound)`) to play the page's exit.
- A `Hero` draws its part at a place that is a function of that same animation.
- When a swipe is released the framework starts no flight on purpose (`HeroController.didPop`: "Don't trigger another flight when a pop is committed as a user gesture back swipe is snapped"), and the method that starts one is private.

## Measured, in the test binding, with the customers list
The avatar's top edge is 76 at the head and 374 in the row.
- **`Hero.transitionOnUserGestures: true`, the built-in switch.** Dragged to a quarter and to half: 88, then 144. The parts leave the head and float over the page that is still shrinking under the finger, across its email line and its buttons. Released at half: 76 in the same frame, then 86, 129, 215, 285, 351, 374 over 450 ms. The parts jump back to the head and travel again. Dragged all the way before release, they are at the row already and nothing jumps.
- **The plain fade-forwards transition in place of the predictive one** (one line in `AppTheme`). During the drag nothing moves: 76. Released: 77, 86, 129, 215, 285, 351, 374, the same trip as from the back button. The page no longer shrinks under the finger, anywhere in the app.
- Both were switched on for the measurement and reverted. Neither was run on a device.

## What that leaves
- As built: a back swipe shows the predictive preview and nothing travels.
- The built-in switch is not usable with Android's predictive back. It fits a swipe whose animation carries on from where the finger left it, as the iOS one does; not tried there.
- Preview during the drag and a flight on release, both: not available from the framework. It needs our own flight for that case or our own handling of a released swipe.
- The owner decides between the preview and the flight on a swipe.

# Part 5 — The number that snapped, and the amount

## Objective
From the owner, after trying it on the Pixel 11: on the way back a deposit's number "comes with the same size as it is and instantly snaps back to the smaller size"; and the amount should travel too, like the avatar and the title. The owner's guess: the framework supports all of it by default.

## 1. Why the number snapped
What travelled was the screen's copy of a part, scaled to the box on its way. In a deposits list the account number is small, regular and grey in the row and the bold, dark title on the screen. The copy that reached the row was the title shrunk to the row's box, and in the next frame the row's own text replaced it. The size was close; the weight and the colour changed at once.

The framework does not blend two widgets by default either: a plain `Hero` draws the destination's widget for the whole trip, which moves the same change to the start.

## 2. A line of text changes style on its way
`EntityHero`'s flight builder now treats a line of text on its own: when both ends are a `Text` on one line, it draws that text under a `DefaultTextStyleTransition` whose `TextStyleTween` goes from the row's style to the screen's with the route's animation. Size and colour change continuously and the weight in steps. All of it is the framework's own classes; ours is the builder that picks the two styles, about thirty lines.
- An avatar, and text that takes two lines at either end, still travel as the screen's copy, scaled.
- For the customer's phone number the `EntityHero` moved inside its `FittedBox`, so that what it wraps is the `Text`.
- The route's animation is at 0 with the row and at 1 with the screen in both directions, so one tween serves the way there and the way back (checked: font sizes 16.0, 16.2, 18.8, 21.5, 22.0 on the way in).

## 3. The amount travels
- The row's amount is an `EntityHero` of a new part, `amount`. It lands in the first figure of the deposit's screen: the principal locked in (`IncomeGenerationGrid`) or the sum invested (`WealthAccumulationGrid`) of a one-time deposit, and the installment (`DetailAmountCard`) of a recurring one. A recurring deposit's "total invested" is not the row's figure and is left alone.
- **A deposit's screen has no placeholder any more.** The amount lands in a section. A section that is a placeholder is rebuilt when the full record arrives, and a destination rebuilt mid-flight ends the flight with two copies on screen. The list's record lacks only the nominees, so the sections are drawn from it at once and the nominees' section arrives with the full record. The customer's screen keeps its placeholder: its list record lacks the savings account as well, and nothing lands in its sections.

## 4. Seen
Storyboards and a film from the test binding with the real font; the profile build is on the owner's Pixel 11. The number and the amount change size, weight and colour along the way in both directions.
