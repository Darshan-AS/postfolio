## How it was checked

The earlier renders had no system bars and no navigation bar, and 360dp is narrower than most phones; the scheme strip's fault only shows at 412dp.

## 1. Monthly Hub

- **Status pill height.** In a row with a button the pill sat 8dp lower than in a row without one: the button brings 4dp of touch padding above and below, and the pill is centred on it. Tests: `rd_hub_view_test.dart` gains a "Monthly Hub spacing" group: the pill's height in rows with and without a button (fails at 10dp without the fix), the 12dp steps, the tile text edge, the hint at a narrow and a roomy width, the gap between two toggles that are on, and the batch bar's button. `list_rows_test.dart` checks the stripe's rectangle and that the row's surface clips it, for each position in a group. # Part 2 — Motion, page transitions and predictive back

## Objective
The owner asked whether Material 3 Expressive animations, page transitions and predictive back gestures can be had from the packages the app already uses, without building features or widgets for them, and to switch on what can. No new dependency, no hand-built transition.

## What was already on
- **Motion tokens**: `AppMotion` reads the official `Springs.expressive*`; the entrance animations (`flutter_animate`) run on them. The community components behind the `App*` wrappers (buttons, button group, FAB menu, navigation bar, loading indicator) bring their own Expressive motion.
- **Page transition on Android**: the official `PredictiveBackPageTransitionsBuilder`, in the theme since 2026-09-02. It follows the finger during a back swipe and plays the fade-forwards transition for a tap.
- **Back handling**: `PopScope` only, no `WillPopScope`. No screen passes `onBack` to `EntityDetailScaffold`, so no detail screen refuses to pop. Detail and form screens are pushed on the root navigator; each tab's navigator holds one page.

## 1. Predictive back on Android 13 to 15
- **The gap.** The theme's builder only draws the animation. Android sends the gesture to Flutter only for an app that opts in with `android:enableOnBackInvokedCallback="true"`, and the manifest never had it. The app targets API 36 (Flutter's default), and Android 16 opts such an app in by itself. On Android 13 to 15 a back swipe arrived as a plain back key: no preview of the screen beneath, no back-to-home animation. The flag is now on `<application>`.
- **The old note.** `tasks.md` said "the last trial had issues between customer detail page to customer list page". That line is from 2026-06-05, before the forms and detail screens moved to push/pop on the root navigator and before the official builder was adopted. The new tests drive exactly that path.
- **Tests**: `test/core/routing/predictive_back_test.dart` (new) signs in through the real router and theme at 412x915 and sends the `flutter/backgesture` messages the Android engine sends. It records `SystemNavigator.setFrameworkHandlesBack`, which tells Android whether the app or the system answers the next swipe.
  - Customer detail: the swipe puts the root navigator into a user gesture (the screen follows the finger), a release lands on `/customers`, and the list takes a tap again.
  - A swipe dragged back to the edge stays on the detail screen, and its back button still works.
  - A form with unsaved input: no preview, and a release opens the discard dialog.
  - An open sheet: the app keeps the swipe, and a release closes the sheet and nothing else.
  - The four tab roots hand the swipe to the system, which plays back-to-home.
  - The manifest carries the flag.
  - Checked that the first test fails when the theme's Android builder is swapped for the zoom transition.
- **Not built, because no package has it**: a preview while a sheet or a dialog is dismissed by the swipe. They close on release without an animation of their own. `material_ui` has predictive back for page routes only. A menu is not closed by back at all (section 5).

## 2. A page transition for every platform
- **iOS.** The theme gave iOS the shared axis transition from `animations`. That builder has no back gesture, and an iPhone has no back button: the only way back was the arrow in the app bar. iOS now takes `material_ui`'s default, the Cupertino transition, which brings the swipe back from the left edge. The theme spreads the package's defaults and names the others, because the Cupertino builder lives in `cupertino_ui`, which the app does not depend on directly.
- **Windows, Linux, Fuchsia.** They were not in the theme and fell back to the Android 10 zoom. That is also what a browser on Windows or Linux showed, since the web build picks the transition from the system it runs on. They now use the horizontal shared axis that macOS already had.
- **Android** is unchanged.
- **Tests**: `test/core/page_transitions_test.dart` (new). For each of the four themes: every `TargetPlatform` is listed, Android has the predictive back builder, the desktop platforms the horizontal shared axis. On iOS a drag from the left edge pops a pushed screen; on macOS, Windows and Linux the same drag does nothing. Before the change the iOS swipe and the four "every platform" cases failed.

## 3. Menus
- `MenuAnchor` has an official open and close animation: the panel grows and its entries fade in one after another, 500 ms to open and 150 ms to close. It is off unless `animated: true` is passed, and no theme turns it on, so it is set where the five menus are built: the overflow menu of a row and of a detail screen, the theme menu, and the passbook and receipt menus.
- **Tests**: `test/core/menu_motion_test.dart` (new) reads `lib/` and fails with the file and line of a `MenuAnchor` without the flag. A widget test opens a detail screen's menu and finds its entry part-way through its fade after 40 ms and opaque once settled. Both fail with the flag taken off one menu.

## 4. Sheets over the navigation bar (found while testing back)
- **What was wrong.** The back-swipe test with an open sheet measured the sort sheet on the Customers tab: it ended at 835dp on a 915dp screen, at the top of the navigation bar. `AppAdaptiveSheet` pushed the sheet on the nearest navigator, and a tab's own screen lives in that tab's navigator, which sits above the bar. So the scrim covered the list only; the navigation bar, the demo banner and the status bar stayed bright, and another tab could be tapped with a modal sheet open, which left the sheet open in the tab that was left. This is as old as the shell: `main` does the same. Sheets opened from a detail screen were never affected, since those screens are on the root navigator.
- **Fix.** `showModalBottomSheet(useRootNavigator: true)`, as `showDialog` already does by default. The scrim covers the whole screen and the sheet reaches the bottom edge. Every sheet closes itself through its own context, so nothing else changes.
- **Test**: `test/core/adaptive_sheet_test.dart` (new) opens a sheet from inside a nested navigator above a bottom bar on a 412x915 window: the sheet ends at 915dp, and a tap where the bar is does not reach the bar. It failed with 835dp before the fix. The router test of a back swipe with a sheet open still passes.

## 5. What the packages do not give
The owner's condition was packages only, so none of this is built. Each is listed in `tasks.md` with what it would take.
- **A transition between the four destinations.** A tab switch is a hard cut. Material's pattern is fade through, and `animations` has `FadeThroughTransition`, but its `PageTransitionSwitcher` disposes the outgoing child: a tab would lose its scroll position and state on every switch. `go_router` exposes the hook (`navigatorContainerBuilder`); the container that keeps all four tabs alive and fades the incoming one is ours to write, about 30 to 50 lines with what an `IndexedStack` does for hidden children (no hit testing, focus or semantics).
- **Back closes an open menu.** Seen on the emulator: with a row's menu open on a tab's own screen, back leaves the app; on a pushed screen it would pop the screen. `MenuAnchor` menus are overlays, not routes, and neither `MenuAnchor` nor `RawMenuAnchor` listens for back (only Escape, a tap outside and a selection close them). This is not new and not caused by the animation or the manifest flag. Closing on back needs a `PopScope` tied to each menu's controller.
- **Predictive back on a sheet.** The official sheet has none. The community `M3EBottomSheet` has a spring entrance and shrinks with the swipe, but it is another sheet, not a setting: half height by default, its own drag and keyboard handling, and, from reading its source (not run), its back gesture ends in `Navigator.pop`, which does not ask a `PopScope` inside the sheet. The payment sheet's unsaved-input guard is one, so every sheet would have to pass its guard as `onDismissRequest`.
- **A spring on the sheet's slide.** `sheetAnimationStyle` takes a curve, but the official sheet is placed by the curve's value, so a spring that overshoots lifts the sheet off the bottom edge for a few frames. Left on the default.
- **Container transform from a row to its screen.** `OpenContainer` (`animations`) and the community card transform push a route of their own, outside `go_router`: no URL, no deep link, and `context.go` from the opened screen acts underneath it. A `Hero` on the avatar needs only the framework, but the shape and size change in flight has to be judged on a device.
- **Seen, not touched**: a row's menu ends about 4dp from the screen's edge in a 412dp test window and looks flush on the 448dp emulator. The same with and without the animation.

## 6. Seen on an emulator
Android 17 (API 37) emulator, 448x997dp, gesture navigation, staging flavor, demo mode. Gestures were sent with `adb shell input motionevent`, holding the swipe to take a screenshot. API 36 and later opt the app in by themselves, so this run says nothing about the manifest flag on Android 13 to 15.
- **Customer detail to the list**: during the swipe the screen shrinks with rounded corners and the list shows behind it; a release lands on the list; dragging back to the edge stays on the detail screen.
- **A tab's own screen**: the swipe shrinks the whole app towards the launcher (the system's back-to-home), and dragging back to the edge keeps the app in front.
- **Customer form with a name typed**: the swipe shows the system's arrow and no preview; a release opens "Discard unsaved changes?".
- **Sort sheet on the Customers tab**: before the fix the scrim stopped at the navigation bar and the demo banner; after it the scrim covers the screen and the sheet reaches the bottom edge. A swipe closes the sheet on release, with no preview.
- **A row's menu**: a screen recording shows the panel growing and WhatsApp, Edit and Delete fading in one after another.

# Part 3 — More of the community package

## Objective
The owner asked for more of `material_3_expressive`: the widgets the comparison marked "switch", plus a split button, chips, the toolbar and refresh on the detail screens, a check of a `Hero` morph for the container transform, and the upgrade of `go_router` and the package. Each widget was first rendered on a throw-away page in the app theme at 360dp, at 1.0x and 2.0x text, with the screen reader's tree printed. A widget was adopted only if it passed, or could be made to pass inside its wrapper.

## 1. Dependencies
- `go_router` 18.0.0 to 18.1.4, `material_3_expressive` 1.2.1 to 1.2.2. The constraints moved too. No code change was needed; the fast tests passed before anything else was touched.

## 2. Adopted
- **Menus** (`AppMenu`, five call sites). Three defects of the community menu are handled in the wrapper:
  - Its width is the anchor's width plus 176dp (224dp from an icon button) and a label that does not fit is cut. "High contrast (Color-blind safe)" was cut at 1.0x. The wrapper measures the labels and opens the menu through `showM3EMenu` with that width, up to the window less 8dp a side.
  - Its ready-made entry sets a semantics label and leaves its text in the tree, so a screen reader gets the label twice. Entries are its free-form item with the content excluded and one label.
  - An open menu is a local history entry of its route. That closes it on back once back reaches Flutter, but it does not make the framework tell Android it handles back, so from a tab's own screen the system still took the gesture. The wrapper holds a `PopScope(canPop: false)` while its menu is open and pops the local history entry itself.
- **Dialogs** (`AppDialog`, used by `AppDialogs`). No fix needed. It opens on the root navigator, names the route with its title, and stacks its actions at 2.0x text. The dialog a sheet becomes at 600dp and wider is still the official one.
- **Refresh on detail screens** (`EntityDetailScaffold.onRefresh`). The wrapper existed; the three screens now pass what to reload.
- **Status bar under the demo banner** (found on the emulator). The community dialog's scrim asks for light status bar icons. In demo mode the banner is under the status bar and nothing asked for dark ones again, so the clock stayed white on the light page. The banner now asks, as an app bar does.
